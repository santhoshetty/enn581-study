Q1 · Forward kinematics of a SCARA armderivecalculate6 marks

A SCARA arm stands on a column of height H = 0.40 m. Joint 1 rotates about the vertical axis of the column. A horizontal link of length a1 = 0.35 m leads to joint 2, which also rotates about a vertical axis. A second horizontal link a2 = 0.25 m carries joint 3, a prismatic joint that extends the tool downwards by d3. The tool frame {T} has zT pointing down and xT along link 2.

(a) Write an elementary transform sequence for \({}^0\mathbf T_T\) and a proximal DH table for the arm. [3]   (b) Evaluate \({}^0\mathbf T_T\) at θ1 = 30°, θ2 = −60°, d3 = 0.10 m. [3]

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) ETS: \(t_z(H)\,R_z(\theta_1)\,t_x(a_1)\,R_z(\theta_2)\,t_x(a_2)\,t_z(-d_3)\,R_x(180^\circ)\) [1 for the order, 1 for the final flip]. Proximal DH (αi−1, ai−1, θi, di): (0, 0, θ1, H), (0, a1, θ2, 0), (180°, a2, 0, d3) [1]. The 180° twist makes z3 point down, so d3 is positive downwards. DH product equals the ETS

(b) Position \(x=a_1c_1+a_2c_{12}=0.3031+0.2165=0.5196\), \(y=a_1s_1+a_2s_{12}=0.175-0.125=0.050\), \(z=H-d_3=0.30\) m [2]. Rotation \(R_z(\theta_1+\theta_2)R_x(180^\circ)\) [1]:

\[ {}^0\mathbf T_T=\begin{bmatrix}0.8660&-0.5000&0&0.5196\\-0.5000&-0.8660&0&0.0500\\0&0&-1&0.3000\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix} \]

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) d3 = H − z [1]. (b) d3 = 0.15 m; c2 = 0.3286; (θ1, θ2) = (−4.689°, 70.818°) or (52.614°, −70.818°) [2]. both reproduce the point

Find the mobility of: (a) a planar five-bar linkage (five links including ground, five revolute joints) [1]; (b) a 3-RPS parallel platform (each leg: revolute at the base, prismatic, spherical at the platform) [2]; (c) a 3-SPS platform (spherical joints at both ends of each prismatic leg). Model answer and marking scheme

(a) 3(5 − 5 − 1) + 5 = 2 [1].

(b) Spatial, D = 6; N = 1 + 1 + 3 × 2 = 8; J = 9; ΣF = 3(1) + 3(1) + 3(3) = 15; M = 6(8 − 9 − 1) + 15 = 3 [2].

Q5 · Jacobian, singularity and static forcederivecalculateexplain7 marks

A planar 2R arm has L1 = 0.5 m, L2 = 0.4 m. (a) Derive its 2×2 position Jacobian and its singular configurations [3]. (b) At q = (30°, 60°), find the joint torques needed to hold a tip force F = (0, −10) N, and explain why τ2 comes out as it does [2]. (c) Find \(\dot{\mathbf q}\) for a tip velocity (0.1, 0) m/s at that pose [2].

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) \(\mathbf J=\begin{bmatrix}-L_1s_1-L_2s_{12}&-L_2s_{12}\\L_1c_1+L_2c_{12}&L_2c_{12}\end{bmatrix}\), det J = L1L2 sin q2. Singular at q2 = 0 (stretched) and π (folded): the boundaries of the workspace [3].

(b) \(\mathbf J=\begin{bmatrix}-0.6500&-0.4000\\0.4330&0\end{bmatrix}\), \(\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf J^\top\mathbf F=(-4.330,\;0)\) N·m, reading F as the force the tip applies to the environment [1]. If F is instead an external load acting on the tip, the holding torques are \(-\mathbf J^\top\mathbf F=(+4.330,\;0)\) N·m. State your reading: either earns the mark. τ2 = 0 because q1 + q2 = 90°: link 2 points straight up, so the vertical force acts along link 2 and has no moment about joint 2 [1].

(c) det J = 0.1732; \(\dot{\mathbf q}=\mathbf J^{-1}(0.1,0)=(0,\;-0.25)\) rad/s [2]. Only joint 2 moves: link 2 is vertical, so rotating it moves the tip horizontally. checked

Q6 · Equations of motion of an RP armderivecalculate8 marks

An RP arm moves in a vertical plane. Joint 1 rotates the arm by θ from the horizontal. Joint 2 slides a point mass m along the arm to distance d from joint 1. The arm itself is massless; gravity acts along −y. (a) Derive the equations of motion by Lagrange and identify M, C (velocity terms) and g [6]. (b) Evaluate τ1 and f2 for m = 2 kg at θ = 30°, d = 0.6 m, \(\dot\theta\) = 0.5 rad/s, \(\dot d\) = 0.2 m/s, \(\ddot\theta\) = 1.0 rad/s², \(\ddot d\) = −0.3 m/s² [2].

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) Position (d cos θ, d sin θ); \(v^2=\dot d^2+d^2\dot\theta^2\) [1]. \(K=\tfrac12m(\dot d^2+d^2\dot\theta^2)\), \(P=mgd\sin\theta\) [1]. Euler–Lagrange [2]:

\[ \tau_1=md^2\ddot\theta+2md\dot d\dot\theta+mgd\cos\theta,\qquad f_2=m\ddot d-md\dot\theta^2+mg\sin\theta \]

M = diag(md², m); C = (2md\(\dot d\dot\theta\) [Coriolis], −md\(\dot\theta^2\) [centrifugal]); g = (mgd cos θ, mg sin θ) [2].

(b) τ1 = 2(0.36)(1.0) + 2(2)(0.6)(0.2)(0.5) + 2(9.81)(0.6)cos 30° = 0.72 + 0.24 + 10.1949 = 11.1549 N·m; f2 = 2(−0.3) − 2(0.6)(0.25) + 2(9.81)(0.5) = −0.6 − 0.3 + 9.81 = 8.9100 N [2]. checked against Lagrange in sympy

Q7 · Partitioned control of one jointderivecalculate7 marks

A joint obeys \(m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=f\) with m = 2 kg, b = 0.5 N·s/m, k = 4 N/m. (a) Write a partitioned control law for trajectory following and the closed-loop error equation [3]. (b) Choose kp, kv for critical damping with ωn = 8 rad/s [1]. (c) Find the steady-state error under a constant 5 N disturbance, and say how to remove it [2]. (d) Compute f at x = 0.10 m, \(\dot x\) = −0.20 m/s when xd = 0.15 m, \(\dot x_d=\ddot x_d=0\) [1].

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) f = αf′ + β with α = m, β = b\(\dot x\) + kx; f′ = \(\ddot x_d+k_v\dot E+k_pE\), E = xd − x; closed loop \(\ddot E+k_v\dot E+k_pE=0\) [3].

(b) kp = ωn² = 64 s⁻², kv = 2ωn = 16 s⁻¹ [1].

(c) |Ess| = fdist/(kpm) = 5/(64 × 2) = 0.0391 m. The sign depends on which way the disturbance pushes: the joint sags in the disturbance’s direction. Add integral action (PID), keeping ki < kvkp = 1024 for stability [2].

(d) E = 0.05, \(\dot E\) = 0.20: f′ = 16(0.2) + 64(0.05) = 6.4 m/s²; f = 2(6.4) + 0.5(−0.2) + 4(0.1) = 13.1 N [1]. checked

Q8 · Differential-drive robotderivecalculateexplain6 marks

Wheels of radius r = 0.08 m sit at (0, ±a) with a = 0.20 m (left wheel 1 at +a). (a) From the generalised wheel model, derive the chassis velocity from the wheel speeds [2]. (b) For \(\dot\varphi\) = (5, 7.5) rad/s find u, θ̇ and the ICR [2]. (c) Find the wheel speeds for u = 0.3 m/s, θ̇ = −0.5 rad/s [1]. (d) Write the robot’s non-holonomic constraint in the inertial frame [1].

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) Rows \(r\dot\varphi_1=u-a\dot\theta\), \(r\dot\varphi_2=u+a\dot\theta\); no-slide gives v = 0; so \(u=r(\dot\varphi_1+\dot\varphi_2)/2\), \(\dot\theta=r(\dot\varphi_2-\dot\varphi_1)/(2a)\) [2].

(b) u = 0.5 m/s, θ̇ = 0.5 rad/s, ICR at (0, u/θ̇) = (0, 1.0) m, i.e. 1 m to the left [2].

(c) \(\dot\varphi_1=(0.3+0.1)/0.08=5.0\), \(\dot\varphi_2=(0.3-0.1)/0.08=2.5\) rad/s [1].

(d) \(-\dot x\sin\theta+\dot y\cos\theta=0\): no sideways velocity, not integrable to a position constraint [1]. checked

Q9 · A* by handcalculateexplain6 marks

Undirected edges with costs: S–A 2, S–B 5, A–C 4, A–D 7, B–D 3, C–G 6, D–G 2. Heuristic h: S 7, A 6, B 4, C 5, D 2, G 0. (a) Run A* from S to G; give the expansion order with g, h, f, and the path and cost [4]. (b) Is h admissible? Consistent? [2]

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) Expand S (g 0, f 7) → open A (2+6 = 8), B (5+4 = 9). Expand A (f 8) → C (6+5 = 11), D (9+2 = 11). Expand B (f 9) → D improves to g 8, f 10 (parent B). Expand D (f 10) → G (10+0 = 10). Expand G (f 10): stop [3]. Path S–B–D–G, cost 10 [1].

(b) True costs to G: S 10, A 9, B 5, C 6, D 2; every h ≤ true, so admissible. Every edge satisfies h(n) ≤ c(n, m) + h(m), so consistent [2]. checked by search

Q10 · Wheeled-robot dynamics (short)explain4 marks

The chassis frame {C} moves with velocity (u, v) and turns at θ̇. Show that the acceleration of the chassis origin, written in {C}, is \((\dot u-v\dot\theta,\;\dot v+u\dot\theta)\), and explain why a robot driving in a circle at constant u needs a sideways force even though v = 0.

Model answer and marking scheme

Velocity in {O}: \(\mathbf v=R(\theta)[u,v]^\top\). Differentiate: \(\dot{\mathbf v}=R(\theta)[\dot u,\dot v]^\top+\dot R(\theta)[u,v]^\top\) with \(\dot R=R\,[\![0,-\dot\theta],[\dot\theta,0]\!]\). In {C}: \((\dot u-v\dot\theta,\;\dot v+u\dot\theta)\) [2]. With v = 0 and constant u, the sideways acceleration is uθ̇ = u²/R: the centripetal acceleration of circular motion. The wheels’ no-slip friction must supply that force [2]. (This is the term printed as vθ̇ on W11 slides 24–25; see the W11 tab.)
