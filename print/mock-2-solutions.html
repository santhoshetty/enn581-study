A four-bar has ground L0 = 0.8 m, input crank L1 = 0.3 m (angle θ1 at {S} = (0, 0)), coupler L2 = 0.7 m, rocker L3 = 0.5 m pivoted at (0.8, 0). For the RRR arm of Sample Q1 (position x = c1r, y = s1r, z = L1 + L2 + L3s2 + L4s23, with r = L3c2 + L4c23): (a) derive the 3×3 position Jacobian [3]; (b) show that det J = −L3L4 sin θ3 · r and interpret each factor [3]; (c) evaluate det J for L3 = 0.4, L4 = 0.3 m at θ = (30°, 20°, 40°) [1].

(c) r = 0.5259 m, det J = −0.12 sin 40° × 0.5259 = −0.04056 [1].

Q7 · PID gains and stabilitycalculateexplain7 marks

A partitioned joint uses \(f'=\ddot x_d+k_v\dot E+k_pE+k_i\int E\,dt\). (a) With kp = 49 s⁻², choose kv for critical damping of the PD part [1]. (b) Derive the third-order error equation and the characteristic polynomial [3]. (c) For which ki is the loop stable? Is ki = 100 stable? [2] (d) What is the steady-state error under a constant disturbance? [1]

Model answer and marking scheme

(b) \(\ddot E+k_v\dot E+k_pE+k_i\!\int\!E=f_{dist}/m\); differentiate: \(\dddot E+k_v\ddot E+k_p\dot E+k_iE=\dot f_{dist}/m\); polynomial \(s^3+14s^2+49s+k_i\) [3].

Q8 · Mecanum (Swedish 45°) robotderivecalculate6 marks

Four wheels (r = 0.05 m) at (±0.20, ±0.15) m all drive along xC (β = 0). Front-left and rear-right rollers have γ = −45°; front-right and rear-left have γ = +45°. (a) Using the generalised model, write the four wheel rows [3]. (b) Find the wheel speeds for pure sideways motion v = 0.2 m/s [2]. (c) Is the robot holonomic? [1]

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) \(r\dot\varphi_i=(u-\dot\theta y_i)+\tan\gamma_i\,(v+\dot\theta x_i)\). Rows (per m/s; divide by r = 0.05): FL (0.2, 0.15, −45°): [20, −20, −7]; FR (0.2, −0.15, +45°): [20, 20, 7]; RL (−0.2, 0.15, +45°): [20, 20, −7]; RR (−0.2, −0.15, −45°): [20, −20, 7] [3].

(b) \(\dot{\boldsymbol\varphi}\) = (−4, 4, 4, −4) rad/s: diagonal pairs turn the same way [2].

(c) The 4×3 matrix has rank 3, so any (u, v, θ̇) is achievable: holonomic [1]. checked

Q9 · Planning conceptsexplain6 marks

(a) Why are grid planners impractical for a 6-DoF arm? Quantify with 100 cells per joint [2]. (b) Compare PRM and RRT: what they build, when to use each, and their completeness [3]. (c) When is A* guaranteed optimal? [1]

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) The node count grows exponentially with dimension: 100⁶ = 10¹² cells (curse of dimensionality) [2].

(b) PRM samples free configurations and connects neighbours into a roadmap, then searches it: multi-query, good for static environments. RRT grows a tree from the start towards random samples: single-query, handles differential constraints. Both are probabilistically complete (success probability tends to 1 as samples grow) and neither is optimal [3].

(c) With an admissible heuristic (h never overestimates). Consistency also lets the closed set stay closed [1].

Q10 · Mechanisms: actuation and G–K failureexplain4 marks

(a) Define fully actuated, under-actuated and (kinematically) redundant, with one example each [2]. (b) A planar body is hung from the ground by three equal parallel links. G–K gives M = 0, yet it moves. Explain [2].

Model answer and marking scheme

(a) Fully actuated: actuators = DoF (6R arm with 6 motors). Under-actuated: fewer actuators than DoF (that arm with 4 motors; or 3-SPS from Mock 1). Redundant: more joint DoF than the task needs (7R arm for a 6-DoF pose) [2].

(b) N = 5, J = 6: 3(5 − 6 − 1) + 6 = 0. G–K assumes independent constraints; with equal parallel links the third link repeats the others’ constraint (one redundant constraint), so the true mobility is 1. Remove one link: 3(4 − 4 − 1) + 4 = 1 [2].
