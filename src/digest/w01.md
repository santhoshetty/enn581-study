## Definitions (slide wording)
- **Mechanism** (p. 21): "a mechanical system that transfers/modifies motion and/or forces from one or more sources to one or more outputs."
- **Kinematic chain** (p. 22): "a system of rigid bodies connected by joints." A mechanism = one or more kinematic chains connecting base to end effector.
- **Link** = rigid structure; **joint** = connection allowing relative motion; **end effector/tool** interacts with the world; **base** rigidly connected to ground (p. 23, nb cell 3).
- **Closed chain** (p. 25): forms a closed loop; **open**: not closed; **serial**: an open chain in which every link except first and last connects to two other links.
- **Serial vs parallel** (p. 24): pictures only (industrial arm vs closed-chain gripper linkage and ABB FlexPicker delta robot); no formal text definition.
- **Closed-loop advantages** (p. 26): actuators at the base make the robot lighter; larger load (mechanical advantage); interesting motion paths (Strandbeest by Theo Jansen; Cyber Tiger, Coros et al. 2013).
- **DOF** (p. 27): "number of independent parameters that define the configuration of a mechanism." Link geometry assumed known; DOF independent of geometry (nb cell 5).
- **Under-actuated** (p. 38): fewer actuators than joints; low cost, simpler design, lower weight.
- **Mobility** (p. 39): same as DOF, the common term for mechanisms; "minimum number of actuators you need in a mechanism".
- **Reachable workspace** (p. 57): region reachable in **one** orientation. **Dexterous**: reachable in **all** possible orientations.
- **Pose** (p. 60): position + orientation.

## Formulas
**G–K first form** (p. 40, nb cell 9):
$$M_{3D}=6(N-1)-5P_1-4P_2-3P_3-2P_4-P_5,\qquad M_{2D}=3(N-1)-2P_1-P_2$$
$M$ mobility/DOF; $N$ links **including grounded link**; $P_i$ number of joints (link pairs) with $i$ DOF. Coefficient = constraints removed ($6-i$ in 3D, $3-i$ in 2D).

**Second form** (p. 41, nb cell 10):
$$M=D(N-J-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{J}F_i=D(N-1)-DJ+\sum_{i=1}^{J}F_i$$
$D=6$ (3D), $3$ (2D); $J$ number of joints; $F_i$ DOF of joint $i$. Meaning: $D(N-1)$ free DOF of moving links; each joint removes $D$; add back $F_i$ allowed.

**Workspace** (p. 57, image): $W_{Dexterous}\subseteq W_{Reachable}$ (nb Q7: $W_R\supseteq W_D$).
**Actuation** (p. 37): controllable = total fully actuated; < under-actuated; > redundant.
**Frames** (p. 62): relating two frames needs 6 parameters (3 translation + 3 rotation), written as a 4x4 homogeneous transformation matrix; no equation in W1.

## Worked examples and numbers
- Point in 2D = 2 DOF; pendulum in 2D = 1 DOF (x and y tied by the link length); rigid body in 3D = 6 DOF (pp. 28–33). nb Q1: rigid body in 2D = 3 DOF, point = 2 (no orientation).
- Two planar links: unjoined 3+3 = 6 DOF; with a revolute joint **4** DOF (pp. 35–36, nb cell 7). nb says the revolute joint also cuts the pendulum mass from 2 to 1.
- **Planar serial RPR** (pp. 42–43, nb cells 11–13): $N=4$ (links 0–3), $P_1=3$, $P_2=0$: $M=3(4-1)-2\times3-1\times0=3$. Method 2: $D=3,N=4,J=3,F=1,1,1$: $M=3(4-3-1)+(1+1+1)=3$.
- **Planar 4-bar** (pp. 44–45, nb Q2): four R joints, $N=4$, $P_1=4$: $M=3(4-1)-2\times4-1\times0=1$. Method 2: $M=3(4-4-1)+(1+1+1+1)=1$.
- **Planar parallel manipulator** (pp. 46–47, nb Q3): triangular platform, three R-P-R legs: $N=8$, $J=9$ (all 1 DOF), $P_1=9$: $M=3(8-1)-2\times9-1\times0=3$. Method 2: $M=3(8-9-1)+9=3$.
- **Stewart platform** (p. 48): homework "What is the mobility of this system?" The slide shows ONE leg only (ground–U–P–S–platform). No answer given.
- **G–K failure** (p. 49, image): three equal parallel legs, each with an R at ground and an R at the platform: $N=5$, $P_1=6$, $P_2=0$: $M=3(5-1)-2\times6-1\times0=\mathbf{0}$; "But the system can move: it has 1 degree of freedom!" Reason: "it assumes that the constraints on motion are independent... the function of the mechanism is not affected even if one of the middle links is removed. It is redundant and should not be counted." p. 50 repeats the figure with no text.
- **Hoberman mechanism** (p. 51): "24 links and 36 revolute joints"; no calculation on the slide.
- Workspace arms (pp. 53–56): PUMA = RRR (Programmable Universal Machine for Assembly); SCARA = RRP (Selective Compliant Articulated Robot for Assembly); cylindrical = RPP (PlateCrane EX); Cartesian = PPP (Yamaha). Figures by A. Becker et al., "Robot Manipulator Workspaces". Workspace set by (1) link geometry, (2) joint constraints/limits (p. 52).
- p. 59 manipulation problems: FK (joint coordinates to end-effector), IK (tip pose to joint coordinates), velocity kinematics (how fast the end effector moves (FK) or should go (IK)), dynamics (torque to accelerate tip; force at tip), trajectory generation (path in joint or tool space).
