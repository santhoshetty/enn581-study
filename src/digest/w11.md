# W11 — Mobile robot dynamics (Wheeled Robots Dynamics), Dr KM Digumarti (40 pages)

Scope warning: the deck contains **no** control content (no feed-forward, look-ahead point, Kanayama/Lyapunov law, PID comparison, tracking error). It is a Newton–Euler derivation of planar chassis dynamics, the C↔O frame conversion, an integration recipe, and two example robots. Pages 1–3 are title, acknowledgement and exam tips; p. 40 is "Next lecture: Planning algorithms". Wheel-torque-to-chassis-force mapping is only shown for the differential drive (p. 37). The Swedish-wheel slide (p. 38) is a figure only.

## Formulas
All "source: slide image" (rendered and read) unless stated.

**F1. Generalised wheel kinematics (recap), p. 6**
$$\dot\varphi_i=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac1{r_i}&\tfrac{\tan\gamma_i}{r_i}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\beta_i&\sin\beta_i\\-\sin\beta_i&\cos\beta_i\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-{}^{C}y_{W_i}\\0&1&{}^{C}x_{W_i}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta&0\\-\sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\dot x\\\dot y\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}$$
φ̇ᵢ wheel angular velocity; rᵢ wheel radius; γᵢ angle between v_free-roll and the normal to the wheel plane; βᵢ angle between v_drive and the chassis; ᶜx_Wᵢ, ᶜy_Wᵢ wheel position in the chassis; θ chassis orientation in the global frame; ẋ, ẏ, θ̇ chassis velocity in the global frame. Source: slide image (p. 5 figure shows ν = ᴼv_C = (ẋ, ẏ, θ̇)ᵀ, wheel frame {Wᵢ}).

**F2. Dynamics problem, p. 9.** $\mathbf Q=f(\boldsymbol\xi,\dot{\boldsymbol\xi},\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi})$, $\boldsymbol\xi=(x,y,\theta)^\top$ pose in the inertial frame, $\mathbf Q=(f_x,f_y,\tau_z)^\top$ net force and torque in an inertial frame. Forward: given Q find trajectory (simulation, analysis; p. 10). Inverse: given trajectory find required force and torque (control; p. 11).

**F3. Setup, p. 12.** {O} global inertial; {C} chassis frame; {G} at centre of mass, same orientation as {C}; m mass; ᴳI_zz moment of inertia about the vertical axis through the CoM. CoM position in {C}: (ᶜx_G, ᶜy_G).

**F4. Force and moment balance, p. 15** (all in {C}; ᶜF_G, ᶜN_G are the inertial force/torque at G; ᶜf_C, ᶜn_C the applied force/torque at C)
$$ {}^{C}F_G-{}^{C}f_C=0\Rightarrow {}^{C}F_x={}^{C}f_x,\ {}^{C}F_y={}^{C}f_y$$
$${}^{C}N_G-{}^{C}n_C-(-{}^{C}P_{CG})\times{}^{C}f_C=0\Rightarrow {}^{C}N_z={}^{C}n_z+({}^{C}y_{CG}\,{}^{C}f_x-{}^{C}x_{CG}\,{}^{C}f_y)$$

**F5. Inertial force and torque, p. 17**
$${}^{C}F_G=m\,{}^{C}\dot v_G\ (F_x=m\,{}^{C}\ddot x_G,\ F_y=m\,{}^{C}\ddot y_G),\quad {}^{C}N_G={}^{G}I\dot\omega+\omega\times{}^{G}I\omega\Rightarrow {}^{C}N_z={}^{G}I\ddot\theta$$
Slide note (handwritten): m and ᴳI are known; ẍ, ÿ, θ̈ are unknown. The ω×Iω term vanishes in planar motion.

**F6. Velocity of G, pp. 19–20**
$${}^{O}\dot P_G={}^{O}\dot P_C+{}^{O}\omega_C\times{}^{O}R_C\,{}^{C}P_G={}^{O}R_C\big[{}^{C}\dot P_C+{}^{C}\omega_C\times{}^{C}P_G\big]$$
(intermediate line uses J(θ) ᶜṖ_C with J = ᴼR_C; ᶜṖ_G = 0 because G is fixed in the chassis). Source: slide image.

**F7. Acceleration of G in {C}, pp. 22, 24**
$${}^{C}\ddot P_G={}^{C}\ddot P_C+{}^{C}\dot\omega_C\times{}^{C}P_{CG}+{}^{C}\omega_C\times{}^{C}\dot P_C+{}^{C}\omega_C\times{}^{C}\omega_C\times{}^{C}P_G$$
With ᶜṖ_C = (u, v, 0), ω = (0, 0, θ̇), ᶜP_G = (ᶜx_G, ᶜy_G, 0); u, v = chassis-frame velocity components of C (u along x̂_C, v along ŷ_C):
$${}^{C}\ddot x_G=\dot u-{}^{C}y_G\ddot\theta-v\dot\theta-{}^{C}x_G\dot\theta^2$$
$${}^{C}\ddot y_G=\dot v+{}^{C}x_G\ddot\theta+v\dot\theta-{}^{C}y_G\dot\theta^2\quad\text{(as printed; see Uncertain U1: should read }u\dot\theta)$$

**F8. Equations of motion (components), p. 25**
$${}^{C}f_x=m(\dot u-{}^{C}y_G\ddot\theta-v\dot\theta-{}^{C}x_G\dot\theta^2),\quad {}^{C}f_y=m(\dot v+{}^{C}x_G\ddot\theta+v\dot\theta-{}^{C}y_G\dot\theta^2)\ \text{(as printed)}$$
$${}^{C}n_z={}^{C}N_z-({}^{C}y_G\,{}^{C}f_x-{}^{C}x_G\,{}^{C}f_y)={}^{G}I\ddot\theta-({}^{C}y_G\,{}^{C}f_x-{}^{C}x_G\,{}^{C}f_y)$$

**F9. Matrix form in the chassis frame, p. 26**
$$\begin{bmatrix}m&0&-m\,{}^Cy_G\\0&m&m\,{}^Cx_G\\-m\,{}^Cy_G&m\,{}^Cx_G&{}^GI+m({}^Cx_G^2+{}^Cy_G^2)\end{bmatrix}\!\begin{bmatrix}\dot u\\\dot v\\\ddot\theta\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}-m\dot\theta(v+{}^Cx_G\dot\theta)\\m\dot\theta(u-{}^Cy_G\dot\theta)\\m\dot\theta({}^Cx_G u+{}^Cy_G v)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{}^Cf_x\\{}^Cf_y\\{}^Cn_z\end{bmatrix}$$
$$\mathbf M\dot\zeta+c(\zeta)={}^{C}\mathbf Q,\quad \zeta=(u,v,\dot\theta)^\top$$
M: depends only on geometry and mass distribution (constant, symmetric; lower-right is the parallel-axis term ᴳI + m(x_G²+y_G²) = inertia about C). c(ζ): function of instantaneous velocity (Coriolis/centripetal). ᶜQ: forces/moments w.r.t. the chassis frame, ᶜQ = (ᶜf_x, ᶜf_y, ᶜn_z)ᵀ. The matrix row 2 and row 3 contain u, which agrees with the cross-product result (not with the printed p. 24/25 ÿ_G). Source: slide image.

**F10. Kinematic link, p. 30.** $\dot{\boldsymbol\xi}=\nu=\mathbf J(\boldsymbol\xi)\zeta$ (from W10; J = ᴼR_C, rotation about z by θ).
$$\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}=\dot{\mathbf J}\zeta+\mathbf J\dot\zeta,\quad \dot\zeta=\mathbf J^{-1}[\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}-\dot{\mathbf J}\zeta]=\mathbf J^{-1}[\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}-\dot{\mathbf J}\mathbf J^{-1}\dot{\boldsymbol\xi}]$$

**F11. Power invariance, p. 32**
$${}^{O}\mathbf Q\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol\xi}={}^{C}\mathbf Q\cdot\zeta\ \Rightarrow\ {}^{O}\mathbf Q^\top\mathbf J\zeta={}^{C}\mathbf Q^\top\zeta\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf J(\boldsymbol\xi)^\top\,{}^{O}\mathbf Q={}^{C}\mathbf Q$$
("conservation of energy (power, to be precise)": power is frame-independent.)

**F12. Inertial-frame equation of motion, p. 34**
$$\mathbf M\big[\mathbf J^{-1}(\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}-\dot{\mathbf J}\mathbf J^{-1}\dot{\boldsymbol\xi})\big]+c(\boldsymbol\xi,\dot{\boldsymbol\xi})=\mathbf J^\top\,{}^{O}\mathbf Q$$
$$(\mathbf J^\top)^{-1}\mathbf M\mathbf J^{-1}\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}+(\mathbf J^\top)^{-1}\big[c(\boldsymbol\xi,\dot{\boldsymbol\xi})-\mathbf M\dot{\mathbf J}\mathbf J^{-1}\dot{\boldsymbol\xi}\big]={}^{O}\mathbf Q\ \Rightarrow\ {}^{O}M\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}+{}^{O}c(\boldsymbol\xi,\dot{\boldsymbol\xi})={}^{O}\mathbf Q$$
So ᴼM = (Jᵀ)⁻¹ M J⁻¹ and ᴼc = (Jᵀ)⁻¹[c − M J̇ J⁻¹ ξ̇]. "Entirely in terms of ξ, ξ̇, ξ̈."

**F13. Forward-simulation recipe, p. 35.** $\dot\zeta=M^{-1}[{}^{C}\mathbf Q-c(\zeta)]$; integrate to get ζ; ξ̇ = J(ξ)ζ; integrate to get ξ. Reason given for using the chassis frame: wheel–ground forces are naturally expressed there.

**F14. Differential-drive wheel forces → chassis wheel-frame wrench, p. 37 (handwritten)**
$$\begin{bmatrix}{}^Cf_x\\{}^Cf_y\\{}^Cn_z\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&0\\-a&a\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}F_1\\F_2\end{bmatrix}$$
3×1 = (3×2)(2×1). Figure: wheel separation 2a, {C, G} coincide at the axle midpoint, F1 on the +ŷ (left) wheel, F2 on the −ŷ (right) wheel, both pointing along +x̂. Check by me: this matches n_z = x f_y − y f_x for forces at (0, ±a) (F1 gives −aF1, F2 gives +aF2). Source: slide image.

## Conventions used
- Frames {O} inertial, {C} chassis (origin at a reference point, not necessarily CoM), {G} at CoM with orientation of {C}. Left superscript = frame of expression (ᶜx_G = G's x-coordinate in {C}); ᴼR_C = rotation of {C} in {O}; notation ᴬT_B/ᴬR_B consistent with the course convention.
- ξ = (x, y, θ)ᵀ inertial pose; ζ = (u, v, θ̇)ᵀ chassis-frame velocity; ν = ξ̇ = J(ξ)ζ with J = ᴼR_C (z-rotation by θ). Note: p. 6 uses the inverse (ᴼ→ᶜ) matrix [cosθ sinθ; −sinθ cosθ], i.e. J⁻¹ = Jᵀ.
- ᶜQ = (ᶜf_x, ᶜf_y, ᶜn_z)ᵀ (applied wrench at C, chassis frame); ᴼQ is the inertial-frame equivalent, related by ᶜQ = Jᵀ ᴼQ. Right-handed, θ anticlockwise from x̂_O, angles in radians. Moment about G of a force at C: −ᶜP_CG × ᶜf_C (p. 15). The slides describe the method as Newton–Euler ("NE"), with Lagrange left as homework.
