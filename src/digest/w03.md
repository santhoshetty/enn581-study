## Formulas
Notation: q joint vector (rad for revolute, m for prismatic), n = number of joints, m = task dimension, L₁, L₂ link lengths (m).

1. Mapping (p. 7, 10, 11): $$\xi_n=\mathcal K(\mathbf q),\qquad \mathbf q=\mathcal K^{-1}(\xi_n)$$ — source: slide text/image. 𝒞 ⊂ 𝕊¹×𝕊¹ for 2 joints (p. 8, "q_i∈𝕊¹ := {s | s∈[0,2π)}"). Task space 𝒯 ∋ ξₙ; ξ may be ⁰Tₙ, {x,y,z,R,P,Y} or {x,y,z,quaternion} (p. 9).
2. Planar RP (p. 24, text; image p. 23 shows the "?" version): $$q_1=\tan^{-1}(y/x),\quad S=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$$ with S = prismatic extension, q₁ = revolute angle from the horizontal. Source: slide text (p. 24 not rendered; p. 23 image checked).
3. Planar RR FK (p. 26; nb cell 15): $$x=L_1\cos q_1+L_2\cos(q_1+q_2),\quad y=L_1\sin q_1+L_2\sin(q_1+q_2)$$ source: notebook + slide image p. 30.
4. Planar RR, q₂ (p. 28; nb cell 15): $$x^2+y^2=L_1^2+L_2^2+2L_1L_2\cos q_2$$ $$\cos q_2=\frac{x^2+y^2-L_1^2-L_2^2}{2L_1L_2},\quad \sin q_2=\pm\sqrt{1-\cos^2 q_2},\quad q_2=\operatorname{atan2}(\sin q_2,\cos q_2)$$ Two solutions (sign of sine) = elbow up / elbow down (nb cell 19). Source: notebook.
5. Compound-angle form (p. 30; nb cell 16): $$x=(L_1+L_2\cos q_2)\cos q_1-(L_2\sin q_2)\sin q_1,\quad y=(L_2\sin q_2)\cos q_1+(L_1+L_2\cos q_2)\sin q_1$$ source: notebook + image.
6. Trig equation (p. 30; nb cell 17): $$A\cos\varphi+B\sin\varphi=C\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi=\tan^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{C}{\pm\sqrt{A^2+B^2-C^2}}\Big)-\tan^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{A}{B}\Big)$$ source: notebook + image.
7. q₁ (p. 30; nb cell 18): $$q_1=\tan^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{y}{x}\Big)-\tan^{-1}\!\Big(\frac{L_2\sin q_2}{L_1+L_2\cos q_2}\Big)$$ Each q₂ gives its own q₁ (two solutions). Source: notebook + image.
8. atan2 (p. 29): atan2(y,x) = tan⁻¹(y/x) if x>0; tan⁻¹(y/x)+π if x<0, y≥0; tan⁻¹(y/x)−π if x<0, y<0; +π/2 if x=0,y>0; −π/2 if x=0,y<0; undefined if x=y=0. Range [−π,π]. Source: slide text (not rendered; layout garbled in text, branches as listed).
9. Geometric RR (p. 31, image): cosine rule in ΔOAB: $$x^2+y^2=L_1^2+L_2^2-2L_1L_2\cos(\pi-q_2)$$ (gives same cos q₂ as item 4). Sine rule: $$\frac{\sin\mu}{L_2}=\frac{\sin(\pi-q_2)}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}=\frac{\sin q_2}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}$$ σ = atan2(y,x); **q₁ = σ − μ**; "two solutions since q₂ has two solutions". Slide says "two solutions from tan⁻¹( )". Source: slide image.
10. Spherical-wrist arm (pp. 32–38, image pp. 35–37): desired pose $${}^0T_E=\begin{bmatrix}R_{3\times3}&T_{3\times1}\\0_{1\times3}&1\end{bmatrix}$$ wrist centre (x_W,y_W,z_W). Step 1 (p. 35): **q₁ = atan2(y_W, x_W)** (view from above). Step 2 (p. 36): r_W² = x_W²+y_W²; $$\cos(\pi-q_3)=\frac{r_W^2+(z_W-H)^2-F^2-G^2}{2FG}$$ H = height of shoulder (joint 2) above base, G = link from joint 2 to joint 3, F = link from joint 3 to wrist centre. Step 3 (p. 37): **q₂ = σ + μ**, σ = atan2(z_W − H, r_W), μ = atan2(F sin q₃, G + F cos q₃). Wrist (p. 38): ³T₆ = three rotations, solve like Euler angles from compound rotations. Source: slide image. Position of wrist centre from the pose (p. 34): slide gives no formula, only "all three wrist joints rotate about the same point".
11. Optimisation form (p. 41; nb cell 24): $$\mathbf q^*=\arg\min_{\mathbf q}\ \lvert\mathcal K(\mathbf q)\ominus\xi^*\rvert$$ G(q) := |𝒦(q) ⊖ ξ*| is the **goal potential** (nb). ⊖ = pose difference operator. Source: notebook + slide.
12. Squared Euclidean goal potential, position only (p. 42, image): $$G(\mathbf q)=(x^*-x)^2+(y^*-y)^2,\ (x,y)=\mathcal K(\mathbf q)$$ source: slide image.
13. Newton–Raphson, scalar (pp. 45–47, image): Taylor $G(q)=G(q^0)+\frac{\partial G}{\partial q}(q^0)(q-q^0)+\text{h.o.t.}$ (drop h.o.t.), set G(q)=0: $$q^{k+1}=q^k-\Big(\frac{\partial G}{\partial q}(q^k)\Big)^{-1}G(q^k)$$ Stopping: $$\Big|\frac{G(q^{k+1})-G(q^k)}{G(q^k)}\Big|\le\epsilon$$ ε arbitrary threshold. Source: slide image.
14. NR for IK (pp. 48–50): x = ℱ(q) ∈ ℝᵐ, q ∈ 𝕊¹×…×𝕊¹ (n times); find q* with G(q*) = ℱ(q*) ⊖ x* = 0. Coordinate Jacobian $$J(\mathbf q^k)=\frac{\partial G}{\partial \mathbf q}(\mathbf q^k)=\frac{\partial\mathcal F}{\partial\mathbf q}(\mathbf q^k)\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$$ If J square and invertible, iterate until G→0. Source: slide text + image p. 46–47 (pp. 48–50 text only).
15. Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse (p. 51, image): full rank; $$J^\dagger=J^\top(JJ^\top)^{-1}\ \text{if }J\text{ fat},n>m\ (\text{infinitely many }\Delta q);\quad J^\dagger=(J^\top J)^{-1}J^\top\ \text{if }J\text{ tall},n<m\ (\text{no exact }\Delta q)$$ Source: slide image.
16. Algorithm (p. 52, image): given x*∈ℝᵐ, q⁰∈ℝⁿ; i=0; e = x* − ℱ(qⁱ); while |e| > ε: **qⁱ⁺¹ = qⁱ + J†(qⁱ) e**; i←i+1. Source: slide image. Note the slide writes G=ℱ⊖x* (sign: current minus target) yet the algorithm uses e = x*−ℱ (target minus current) with a plus sign: consistent, as e = −G.

## Worked examples and numbers on the slides
- Planar 2R workspace (pp. 14–16): L₁ = 1 m, L₂ = 0.7 m, q₁,q₂ ∈ [0,2π): reachable workspace is the annulus with radii |L₁−L₂| and L₁+L₂ (symbolic labels on p. 15; 0.3 m and 1.7 m follow trivially); dexterous workspace is the empty set. Joints limited to [0,π/2]: reachable region is a crescent (p. 16), dexterous still empty.
- Planar RP (p. 24): S = √(x²+y²), q₁ = tan⁻¹(y/x). RR (pp. 25–31) and 6-DoF (pp. 32–38) are symbolic only. No numeric Newton–Raphson example on the slides.
- p. 33: pose = 6 numbers, 6 joints, so no redundant solutions. p. 56: any 6-DoF single chain of R/P joints is "solvable" but may lack an analytic form; sufficient for closed form: three neighbouring joint axes intersect.
- p. 44: G(q)=0 not always achievable (ξ* unreachable); solution not always found (local optimum); not unique (initial condition, redundancy).
- p. 54: closed-form faster, all solutions known a priori, harder as joints increase; numerical suits large chains and gives a close-enough answer when no exact one exists.
- Continuum robots (p. 59): actuator space q (cable length) →f_specific→ configuration space (κ, φ, ℓ arc parameters) →f_independent→ task space; configuration space differs from actuator space.

## Conventions used
- Joint variable q_i (revolute angle, prismatic extent S); poses ξ, ξₙ; ⁰T_E for end-effector-in-base transform; ⊖ denotes pose difference. The deck does **not** use DH parameters in this week; frames are only the base xyz (red axes) in the 6-DoF figures, with z up, q₁ about z.
- Planar RR: q₁ measured from the horizontal x axis, q₂ measured relative to link 1 (a relative joint angle, so the end-effector direction is q₁+q₂). Angles in radians (𝕊¹ = [0,2π)).
- In the 6-DoF figures q₁ about the vertical z axis, q₂ and q₃ about horizontal axes in the arm's vertical plane; the notebook ETS code uses Rz(), Rx(), Ry(), tz(), tx() (Corke's `rtb.ET`).
