## Formulas
**F1. Chassis ↔ inertial velocity** (p13–14, source: slide image)
$$\nu=\begin{bmatrix}\dot x\\\dot y\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}=\mathbf J(\theta)\,\zeta=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta&0\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}$$
ẋ, ẏ: chassis velocity in {O} (m/s); θ̇ rad/s; u, v: chassis velocity along x̂_C, ŷ_C (m/s); ζ = ᶜv_C = (u, v, θ̇) is "velocity in the robot frame"; ν = ᴼv_C is "derivatives of generalised coordinates". θ(t) = angle of x̂_C from x̂_O (CCW positive in figure). Inverse: ζ = J⁻¹(θ)ν (p16). The slides write J(θ) and later R(θ)/R⁻¹(θ) for the same rotation (p68).

**F2. Wheel-to-chassis relation** (p17): ζ = Wφ̇, with φ̇ the vector of wheel angular velocities (rad/s); W depends on wheel configuration (source: text).

**F3. Omni wheel in its own frame {W}** (pp22–25, image). x̂_W = driving direction, ŷ_W = normal to wheel plane (axle direction as drawn). Free-roll direction is ŷ_W rotated about by γ, pointing up-left in the figure.
$$\begin{bmatrix}v_x\\v_y\end{bmatrix}=v_{drive}\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}+v_{free\text{-}roll}\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\gamma\\\cos\gamma\end{bmatrix},\quad v_{drive}=v_x+v_y\tan\gamma,\quad v_{free\text{-}roll}=\frac{v_y}{\cos\gamma}$$
$$\dot\varphi=\frac{v_{drive}}{r}=\frac{v_x+v_y\tan\gamma}{r}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac1{r_i}&\tfrac{\tan\gamma_i}{r_i}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}v_x\\v_y\end{bmatrix}$$
r: wheel radius (m). Only v_drive is tied to φ̇; v_free-roll is unconstrained.

**F4. Step 1, wheel velocity in chassis axes** (p28, image): ᶜv_Wi = R(β_i)ᵂⁱv, i.e. $\begin{bmatrix}\cos\beta_i&-\sin\beta_i\\\sin\beta_i&\cos\beta_i\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{}^{W_i}v_x\\{}^{W_i}v_y\end{bmatrix}$. β_i: angle of the wheel's driving direction (x̂_Wi) measured from the chassis x̂_C (figure p32: red line parallel to x̂_C, β_i CCW to x̂_Wi).

**F5. Step 2, rigid-body velocity of the wheel point** (p30, image)
$$ {}^{C}v_{W_i}=\begin{bmatrix}u-\dot\theta\,{}^Cy_{W_i}\\ v+\dot\theta\,{}^Cx_{W_i}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-{}^Cy_{W_i}\\0&1&{}^Cx_{W_i}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}$$
ᶜx_Wi, ᶜy_Wi: wheel position in {C} (m).

**F6. Combine** (p32, p34, image): $R(\beta_i)\,{}^{W_i}v=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-{}^Cy\\0&1&{}^Cx\end{bmatrix}\zeta$, so ${}^{W_i}v=R(\beta_i)^{T}(\dots)\zeta$ (slide writes the transpose explicitly as [cosβ sinβ; −sinβ cosβ]).

**F7. Generalised wheeled-robot model** (p36, image; the slide's headline result)
$$\dot\varphi_i=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac1{r_i}&\tfrac{\tan\gamma_i}{r_i}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\beta_i&\sin\beta_i\\-\sin\beta_i&\cos\beta_i\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-{}^Cy_{W_i}\\0&1&{}^Cx_{W_i}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta&0\\-\sin\theta&\cos\theta&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\dot x\\\dot y\\\dot\theta\end{bmatrix}$$
Last 3×3 = R⁻¹(θ) = Rᵀ(θ) (inertial → chassis); next 2×3 = rigid-body Jacobian of the wheel point; next 2×2 = Rᵀ(β_i) (chassis → wheel axes); then row [1/r, tanγ/r] picks v_drive. Symbol definitions on p36: φ̇_i wheel angular velocity; r_i radius; **γ_i "the angle between v_free-roll and the normal to the plane of the wheel"**; **β_i "the angle between the v_drive and the robot chassis"**; ᶜx_Wi, ᶜy_Wi wheel position w.r.t. chassis; θ chassis orientation in global frame.

**F8. Fixed standard wheel** (p39, image/text): same as F7 with row [1/r_i, 0] (γ_i = 0, "technically γ is not defined; this wheel cannot slide sideways"), **β_i = constant**.
**F9. Steerable standard wheel** (p42): identical with **β_i(t) function of time**; γ_i = 0.

**F10. Constraint form for standard wheels** (p68, image; "These are non-holonomic constraints")
$$r_i\dot\varphi_i=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\beta_i&\sin\beta_i&-{}^Cy_{W_i}\cos\beta_i+{}^Cx_{W_i}\sin\beta_i\end{bmatrix}R^{-1}(\theta)\nu\quad\text{(rolling)}$$
$$0=\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\beta_i&\cos\beta_i&{}^Cy_{W_i}\sin\beta_i+{}^Cx_{W_i}\cos\beta_i\end{bmatrix}R^{-1}(\theta)\nu\quad\text{(no sliding)}$$
I checked both rows by multiplying out F7 with γ=0.

**F11. Manoeuvrability** (p63): δ_M = δ_m + δ_s. "Mobility – think of this as the number of driven wheels; steerability – the number of steerable wheels" (informal slide wording, text).

**F12. Holonomic** (p66): f(β, γ, φ, θ, x, y) = 0, position only, no derivatives. **Non-holonomic** (p67): contains derivatives of position that cannot be integrated to position-only form ("non-integrable").

## Worked examples and numbers on the slides
**No numerical values appear anywhere in the lecture** (all symbolic). Worked cases:
- **Differential drive** (pp44–47, image): wheels radius r; 2a = wheel separation (track); W1 at ᶜy=+a, W2 at ᶜy=−a, both ᶜx=0; γ=0, β=0. Rows: φ̇1 = (1/r)(u − aθ̇), φ̇2 = (1/r)(u + aθ̇) (via [1/r 0][I2][1 0 ∓a]). W⁻¹ (2×3, "2×3" handwritten) = [[1/r, 0, −a/r],[1/r, 0, a/r]]. Slide gives **ζ = Wφ̇ with W (3×2) = [[r/2, r/2],[0, 0],[−r/(2a), r/(2a)]]**, so u = r(φ̇1+φ̇2)/2, v = 0, θ̇ = r(φ̇2−φ̇1)/(2a).
- **Skid-steer, 4 standard wheels** (pp49–52, image): 2a × 2b rectangle; W1 (−b, a), W2 (b, a), W3 (−b, −a), W4 (b, −a); r, γ=0, β=0 for all. 4×3 matrix rows (1/r)[1, 0, −a] for W1, W2 and (1/r)[1, 0, +a] for W3, W4 (b drops out of every row). 3×4 inverse: u = (r/4)Σφ̇_i, v = 0, θ̇ = (r/4a)(−φ̇1−φ̇2+φ̇3+φ̇4). My check: this is the pseudo-inverse of the 4×3 matrix, though the slide does not say so.
- **Tricycle** (pp56–58, image): front wheel W1 driven and steered, at (ᶜx, ᶜy) = (b, 0), r, γ=0, β1=β1(t); rear wheels W2, W3 not driven, fixed. Rear wheels cannot slide, so **v = 0**. Step 2: ᶜv_W1 = (u, bθ̇). Step 1: ᶜv_W1 = R(β1)(rφ̇1, 0). Result **φ̇1 = (1/r)√(u² + b²θ̇²)** and **β1 = atan2(bθ̇, u)** (p58). The slide does not print a derivation line, only the result. The derivation works only if the rear axle passes through C (rear wheels at x=0); the slide does not state this.
- **Ackermann** (p61, figure): rear wheels fixed, front wheels steered at different angles so that all wheel axle-lines meet at one point, the **instantaneous centre of rotation (ICR)**. Stated purpose: "make sure that all the wheels rotate without slipping".
- **Manoeuvrability examples** (p64, image): (1) 2 fixed wheels + castor: δm=2, δs=0, δM=2. (2) 2 fixed wheels + 1 steered standard wheel: δm=1, δs=1, δM=2. (3) 2 steered standard wheels + 1 Swedish wheel: δm=1, δs=2, δM=3. (4) triangle with 3 Swedish wheels: δm=3, δs=0, δM=3. The labels are the slide's; the text does not explain how each was counted.
- **Triangular 3-Swedish-wheel robot** (p54, image zoomed): {C} at centre, d = centre-to-vertex distance; W1 at top (0, d), W2 bottom-right, W3 bottom-left (equilateral). Each wheel has two arrows (drive along the axle tangent, free-roll radial). Slide gives no equations. This is Practical Q1.
- **4-Swedish-wheel robot** (p55, figure only): w and ℓ labelled on the chassis; two wheel rows, arrows show drive (±x̂) and slanted roller directions. No equations.

## Conventions used
- Right-handed planar frames; θ(t) CCW from x̂_O to x̂_C. {W_i} has x̂ = driving direction, ŷ = wheel-plane normal (axle). No DH or ᴬT_B appears this week; ᶜv_Wi / ᵂⁱv notation uses a leading superscript for the frame of expression.
- β_i from chassis x̂_C to the wheel drive direction (CCW positive, p32 figure). γ_i from ŷ_W (wheel-plane normal) to v_free-roll, CCW (gives (−sinγ, cosγ)); γ=0 for all standard wheels and for an omni wheel whose rollers are ⟂ to the driving direction (Practical Q1's case).
- Chassis position of wheel (ᶜx, ᶜy) in chassis axes, +x forward, +y left. Differential drive: W1 at +a (left), W2 at −a (right). "2a" = track width.
- Rotation matrix R(θ) = J(θ); inertial→chassis uses R⁻¹(θ)=Rᵀ(θ). Angles in radians, no degrees on slides.
