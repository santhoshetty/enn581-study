## Formulas
**Lagrangian** (p. 16; source: slide image, nb cell 9): $$\mathcal L(\mathbf q,\dot{\mathbf q})=K(\mathbf q,\dot{\mathbf q})-P(\mathbf q)$$ K, P scalars (J). The slide handwrites "L = K − P".

**Euler–Lagrange** (p. 17; slide image, nb cell 10): $$\mathbf Q=\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot{\mathbf q}}\right)-\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\mathbf q}$$ Q = joint torques (N m) for revolute, forces (N) for prismatic.

**General EoM** (p. 25; slide image): $$\mathbf Q=\mathbf M(\mathbf q)\ddot{\mathbf q}+\mathbf C(\mathbf q,\dot{\mathbf q})+\mathbf g(\mathbf q)$$ Sizes n×1 = (n×n)(n×1) + n×1 + n×1 (handwritten on slide). M mass/joint-space inertia matrix, C velocity-product term, g gravity term.
**Extended** (p. 26; slide image, nb cell 26): $$\mathbf Q=\mathbf M\ddot{\mathbf q}+\mathbf C+\mathbf g+\mathbf f(\dot{\mathbf q})+\mathbf J^\top(\mathbf q)\mathbf w$$ f friction, w external wrench (e.g. contacts); other potential energy (e.g. joint springs) lands in g.

**Properties of M** (p. 28): symmetric, positive definite; diagonal = inertia seen by joint j; off-diagonals = products of inertia coupling joint j's acceleration to joint i's torque. Notebook cell 31: square, positive definite, hence always invertible.

**C split** (p. 30; slide image): $$\mathbf C(\mathbf q,\dot{\mathbf q})=C(\mathbf q)[\dot{\mathbf q}^2]+B(\mathbf q)[\dot{\mathbf q}\dot{\mathbf q}]$$ Centripetal = squared velocities (C(q) n×n acting on [q̇ᵢ²]); Coriolis = products (B(q) n×n(n−1)/2 acting on the n(n−1)/2 products q̇ᵢq̇ⱼ, i<j; handwritten, n=3 example). Cites Lynch & Park §8.1.

**Task-space** (p. 32): $$\mathcal F=\mathbf M_x\ddot{\mathbf x}+\mathbf C_x+\mathbf g_x,\qquad \mathbf Q=\mathbf J^\top(\mathbf q)\mathcal F$$

**Forward dynamics** (p. 48; nb cell 30): $$\ddot{\mathbf q}=\mathbf M^{-1}\big(\mathbf Q-\mathbf C-\mathbf g-\mathbf f-\mathbf J^\top\mathbf w\big)$$ (slide p. 48 gives the same formula.)

**Newton / Euler** (p. 34; slide image): $$F=m\,\dot{\upsilon}_C,\qquad N={}^C\!I\,\dot\omega+\omega\times{}^C\!I\,\omega$$ F force (N) on centre of mass; N moment (N m) about centre of mass; ω, ω̇ link angular velocity/acceleration; ᶜI inertia tensor about the COM frame (slide handwrites "I → inertia").

**Inertia tensor** (p. 36; slide image; uniform density ρ, dv=dx dy dz): $$^AI=\begin{bmatrix}I_{xx}&I_{xy}&I_{xz}\\I_{xy}&I_{yy}&I_{yz}\\I_{xz}&I_{yz}&I_{zz}\end{bmatrix}$$ $I_{xx}=\iiint(y^2+z^2)\rho\,dv$, $I_{yy}=\iiint(x^2+z^2)\rho\,dv$, $I_{zz}=\iiint(x^2+y^2)\rho\,dv$; $I_{xy}=-\iiint xy\rho\,dv$, $I_{yz}=-\iiint yz\rho\,dv$, $I_{xz}=-\iiint xz\rho\,dv$. Symmetric; **depends on the frame {A}** ("the frame of reference is important", p. 39).

**Cuboid, frame at a corner** (pp. 37–38; x spans W, y spans L, z spans H, limits 0..W, 0..L, 0..H): $I_{xx}=m(L^2+H^2)/3$; $I_{yy}=m(W^2+H^2)/3$; $I_{zz}=m(L^2+W^2)/3$; off-diagonals $-mWL/4$ (xy), $-mHL/4$ (yz), $-mHW/4$ (xz). The slide writes the integral $I_{xy}=\iiint xy\rho\,dv=m(WL)/4$ without the minus; the matrix carries the minus (slide p. 37 vs 38: see Uncertain).
**Cuboid, frame at centroid** (p. 39): diagonal $m(L^2+H^2)/12$, $m(W^2+H^2)/12$, $m(L^2+W^2)/12$; all off-diagonals 0.

**Parallel-axis theorem — NOT on the slides** (external, standard; check vs Craig/Lynch): $^AI={}^CI+m\big(\mathbf p^\top\mathbf p\,\mathbf 1_3-\mathbf p\mathbf p^\top\big)$, p = COM position in {A}. Consistency with p. 38–39: p=(W/2,L/2,H/2) gives $I_{xx}=m(L^2+H^2)/12+m(L^2/4+H^2/4)=m(L^2+H^2)/3$ and $I_{xy}=0-m(W/2)(L/2)=-mWL/4$. Matches slides.

**Newton–Euler inward balances** (p. 45; slide image). Fᵢ, Nᵢ: Newton/Euler force/moment at {Cᵢ}; fᵢ, nᵢ: force/moment on link i from link i−1 at origin {i}; P_Cᵢ COM, Pᵢ₊₁ next origin, both in {i}. $$^iF_i={}^if_i-{}^iT_{i+1}\,{}^{i+1}f_{i+1}$$ $$^iN_i={}^in_i-{}^in_{i+1}+(-{}^iP_{C_i})\times{}^if_i-({}^iP_{i+1}-{}^iP_{C_i})\times{}^if_{i+1}$$ (slide writes "ᵢTᵢ₊₁" where a rotation ⁱRᵢ₊₁ is meant, and omits rotating nᵢ₊₁; flagged.)

**Recursions as coded in the scripts** (source: `rp_newton_euler.py`, Craig convention, Z=[0 0 1]ᵀ, R = ⁱ⁻¹ᵢR):
- Outward, revolute i: $^i\omega_i=R^\top\omega_{i-1}+\dot q_iZ$; $^i\dot\omega_i=R^\top\dot\omega_{i-1}+(R^\top\omega_{i-1})\times\dot q_iZ+\ddot q_iZ$; $^i\dot v_i=R^\top(\dot\omega_{i-1}\times P_i+\omega_{i-1}\times(\omega_{i-1}\times P_i)+\dot v_{i-1})$.
- Outward, prismatic i: $\omega_i=R^\top\omega_{i-1}$, $\dot\omega_i=R^\top\dot\omega_{i-1}$, $\dot v_i=R^\top(\cdots)+2\,\omega_i\times\dot d_iZ+\ddot d_iZ$ (last-but-one term = Coriolis).
- COM: $\dot v_{C_i}=\dot\omega_i\times P_{C_i}+\omega_i\times(\omega_i\times P_{C_i})+\dot v_i$; $F_i=m_i\dot v_{C_i}$; $N_i=I_i\dot\omega_i+\omega_i\times I_i\omega_i$.
- Inward (i = n…1; f_{n+1}=n_{n+1}=0): $f_i=R_{i+1}f_{i+1}+F_i$; $n_i=N_i+R_{i+1}n_{i+1}+P_{C_i}\times F_i+P_{i+1}\times R_{i+1}f_{i+1}$; τᵢ = nᵢ·Z (revolute) or fᵢ·Z (prismatic).
- Gravity trick: set $\dot v_0=[0,g,0]^\top$ (base accelerates up) instead of a separate gravity term.
- Extract M, V, G from one routine: G = NE(q̇=q̈=0, gravity on); M[:,j] = NE(gravity off, q̇=0, q̈=e_j); V = NE(gravity off, q̈=0).

**Computational cost** (p. 47): NE: 104n − 77 mult., 92n − 70 add. Lagrangian: 32n⁴+86n³+171n²+53n−128 mult., 25n⁴+66n³+129n²+42n−96 add. n=6 (slide): 547 vs 66394 mult.; 482 vs 51456 add. (handwritten "ne", "l"). Source: Handbook of Industrial Robotics. NE is O(n), Lagrangian O(n⁴).

## Conventions used
Q = joint force/torque vector; leading superscript = frame; ᶜI = inertia about COM frame; υ linear, ω angular velocity. NE uses Craig-style frames (joint axis = z). 2R: q₂ relative to link 1, gravity −y, point masses at link tips (L₁, L₂ = COM distances). No DH table on these slides.
