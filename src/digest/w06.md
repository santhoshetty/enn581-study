## Formulas
(Source tags: NB = notebook cell, IMG = slide image checked, TXT = slide text unverified.)

1. Skew operator, $\mathbf s\in\mathbb R^3\mapsto\mathbf S\in so(3)$:
$$[\mathbf s]_\times=\begin{pmatrix}0&-s_3&s_2\\ s_3&0&-s_1\\ -s_2&s_1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathbf s=\vee_\times(\mathbf S)$$ p. 10; NB cell 6.
2. Augmented skew, $\hat{\mathbf s}=(s_1..s_6)^\top\in\mathbb R^6\mapsto\hat{\mathbf S}\in se(3)$:
$$\hat{\mathbf S}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-s_6&s_5&s_1\\ s_6&0&-s_4&s_2\\ -s_5&s_4&0&s_3\\ 0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}$$ i.e. s1-s3 linear (translation), s4-s6 angular. p. 12; NB cell 7.
3. ETS: $^0\mathbf T_E=\prod_{i=1}^{M}\mathbf E_i(\eta_i)$; $\eta_i$ constant or joint coordinate, M elementary transforms. p. 13; NB cell 20.
4. Chain rule: $\dot{\mathbf T}=\frac{d\mathbf T}{dt}=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\partial\mathbf T}{\partial q_j}\dot q_j$; $\mathbf T\in SE(3)$, $\dot{\mathbf T}$ and $\partial\mathbf T/\partial q_j\in\mathbb R^{4\times4}$. pp. 15-16; NB 21. Blocks: $\partial\mathbf T/\partial q_j=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf J_{R_j}&\mathbf J_{t_j}\\ \mathbf 0&\mathbf 0\end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf J_{R_j}\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}$, $\mathbf J_{t_j}\in\mathbb R^{3\times1}$ (p. 15). Hence $\dot{\mathbf R}=\sum\mathbf J_{R_j}\dot q_j$, $\dot{\mathbf t}=\sum\mathbf J_{t_j}\dot q_j$.
5. Translation: $\boldsymbol\upsilon=\dot{\mathbf t}=\mathbf J_\upsilon(\mathbf q)\dot{\mathbf q}$, $\mathbf J_\upsilon=[\mathbf J_{t_1}\cdots\mathbf J_{t_n}]$, column $j$: $\tau\!\big(\partial\mathbf T/\partial q_j\big)$ ($\tau$ = translation part, 3×1). pp. 17, 26; NB 30-31.
6. Rotation: $\dot{\mathbf R}=[\boldsymbol\omega]_\times\mathbf R$; post-multiply by $\mathbf R^\top$ → $[\boldsymbol\omega]_\times=\sum(\mathbf J_{R_j}\mathbf R^\top)\dot q_j$; so
$$\boldsymbol\omega=\mathbf J_\omega\dot{\mathbf q},\quad \mathbf J_{\omega_j}=\vee_\times\!\Big(\rho\big(\tfrac{\partial\mathbf T}{\partial q_j}\big)\rho(\mathbf T)^\top\Big)$$ ρ = rotation part (3×3). pp. 18-19, 26; NB 33-34. Single-joint form: $d\mathbf R/d\theta=[\hat{\boldsymbol\omega}]_\times\mathbf R$, $\hat{\boldsymbol\omega}=\vee_\times(\dot{\mathbf R}\mathbf R^\top)$ (NB 13 only).
7. Manipulator Jacobian: $^0\boldsymbol\nu=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol\upsilon\\ \boldsymbol\omega\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf J_\upsilon\\ \mathbf J_\omega\end{pmatrix}\dot{\mathbf q}={}^0\mathbf J(\mathbf q)\dot{\mathbf q}$; ν = spatial velocity / twist (6×1), J is 6×n, q̇ is n×1; υ, ω expressed in {0}. pp. 20-21 ("Pay attention to the frame"); NB 36.
8. Elementary derivatives (p. 29, 31; NB 13, 16). Translation: $\partial\mathbf T_{t_x}(d)/\partial d=\hat{\mathbf t}_x$ = zeros with a single 1 at entry (1,4); $\hat{\mathbf t}_y$: 1 at (2,4); $\hat{\mathbf t}_z$: 1 at (3,4). Rotation: $\partial\mathbf T_{R_x}/\partial\theta=\hat{\mathbf R}_x\mathbf T_{R_x}(\theta)$ with $\hat{\mathbf R}_x$ having −1 at (2,3), +1 at (3,2); $\hat{\mathbf R}_y$: +1 at (1,3), −1 at (3,1); $\hat{\mathbf R}_z$: −1 at (1,2), +1 at (2,1). Sign-flip rule (NB only): if positive joint motion is negative about/along the axis, use $\mathbf T^\top$-version for rotations (code: `Rhat.T @ T.T`) and $-[\hat{\mathbf t}]$ for translations.
9. Derivative of the whole ETS (p. 32; NB 22): $\frac{\partial\mathbf T}{\partial q_j}=\prod_{i=1}^{\mu(j)-1}\mathbf E_i\;\frac{\partial\mathbf E_{\mu(j)}(q_j)}{\partial q_j}\;\prod_{i=\mu(j)+1}^{M}\mathbf E_i$; μ(j) = index of the elementary transform holding joint j. (Slide p. 32 writes the first product to $\mathbf E_n(\eta_n)$ instead of $\mathbf E_M$: notation slip.)
10. Change of Jacobian frame (p. 40 IMG; NB cell 38): from $^B\mathbf J$ to $^A\mathbf J=\begin{pmatrix}{}^A\mathbf R_B&\mathbf 0_{3\times3}\\ \mathbf 0_{3\times3}&{}^A\mathbf R_B\end{pmatrix}{}^B\mathbf J$. (Notebook writes $^A\mathbf R_0$ with B=0.)
11. Inverse velocity: $\dot{\mathbf q}=\mathbf J^{-1}(\mathbf q)\,\boldsymbol\nu$ (p. 41); questions on p. 42: square? else pseudo-inverse; non-singular? invertible for all q?
12. Singularity: $\det\mathbf J(\mathbf q)=0$ (pp. 44; NB 45). Boundary: arm stretched out or folded; interior: e.g. wrist joints line up.
13. Manipulability ellipsoid (pp. 51-53): unit joint-velocity ball $\dot{\mathbf q}\dot{\mathbf q}^\top=1$ (as written; $\dot{\mathbf q}^\top\dot{\mathbf q}=1$ meant) maps via ν = J q̇ to
$$\boldsymbol\nu^\top\big(\mathbf J\mathbf J^\top\big)^{-1}\boldsymbol\nu\le1$$ boundary = equality. With $A=\mathbf J\mathbf J^\top$, eigenvalues λ_i, eigenvectors v_i: semi-axes along v_i of length $\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ (p. 53 figure).
14. Manipulability measure (p. 55 IMG): $m:=\sqrt{\lambda_{min}(A)}/\sqrt{\lambda_{max}(A)}$, $0\le m\le1$; m=1 isotropic, m=0 singular (ellipse collapsed in one dimension). Slide note: some textbooks define the reciprocal as m.
15. Statics (p. 58): wrench $\mathbf F_E=(F_x,F_y,F_z,n_x,n_y,n_z)^\top$ (forces N, moments N·m) and joint torques $\boldsymbol\tau$: $\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf J^\top(\mathbf q)\mathbf F_E$. Frame of $\mathbf F_E$ not stated (presumably the one J is in).
16. Force ellipsoid (p. 59 IMG): $\mathbf F^\top\big(\mathbf J\mathbf J^\top\big)\mathbf F\le1$ — note NO inverse (velocity ellipsoid has the inverse). Same principal axes as the velocity ellipsoid; axis lengths reciprocal.

### Cross-product note (full transcription; Cross_Product_Clarification.pdf, 1 p., IMG checked)
Title: "Clarifying the Cross Product in Craig's Velocity Formula". "In Craig's robotics book, the recursive velocity formula includes the term: $v_{i+1}=v_i+\omega_i\times P_{i+1}$. At first glance, ω × P may seem confusing if read as a direct multiplication of two 3×1 vectors. In fact, it is the standard 3D vector cross product. Below are two equivalent ways to write it."
1. Determinant form: for $\omega=[\omega_x,\omega_y,\omega_z]^T$, $P=[P_x,P_y,P_z]^T$: $\omega\times P=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\ \omega_x&\omega_y&\omega_z\\ P_x&P_y&P_z\end{vmatrix}$; "Expanding this determinant gives: $\omega\times P=(\omega_yP_z-\omega_zP_y)\hat i+(\omega_xP_z-\omega_zP_x)\hat j+(\omega_xP_y-\omega_yP_x)\hat k$."
2. Skew-symmetric form: $[\omega]=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\omega_z&\omega_y\\ \omega_z&0&-\omega_x\\ -\omega_y&\omega_x&0\end{bmatrix}$; "Then the cross product is simply: ω × P = [ω] P".
Takeaway: "Craig's ω × P is just the ordinary vector cross product. It can be written either in determinant form (as we first learn in vector calculus) or in skew-symmetric matrix form (which is more convenient in robotics derivations)."
**Error in the note**: the $\hat j$ term as printed, $(\omega_xP_z-\omega_zP_x)$, has the wrong sign; the determinant expansion gives $(\omega_zP_x-\omega_xP_z)\hat j$. The skew-matrix form is correct (its 2nd row gives $\omega_zP_x-\omega_xP_z$), so the note's two forms disagree as printed.

### Textbook background (NOT on slides; standard Craig; reviewer to confirm before use)
Outward recursion, link i→i+1, vectors in {i+1}: revolute: $^{i+1}\omega_{i+1}={}^{i+1}_iR\,{}^i\omega_i+\dot\theta_{i+1}{}^{i+1}\hat Z_{i+1}$; $^{i+1}v_{i+1}={}^{i+1}_iR\big({}^iv_i+{}^i\omega_i\times{}^iP_{i+1}\big)$. Prismatic: $^{i+1}\omega_{i+1}={}^{i+1}_iR\,{}^i\omega_i$; $^{i+1}v_{i+1}={}^{i+1}_iR({}^iv_i+{}^i\omega_i\times{}^iP_{i+1})+\dot d_{i+1}{}^{i+1}\hat Z_{i+1}$. Geometric columns: revolute $\begin{pmatrix}z_{j-1}\times(p_e-p_{j-1})\\ z_{j-1}\end{pmatrix}$, prismatic $\begin{pmatrix}z_{j-1}\\ 0\end{pmatrix}$. The note drops the rotation matrix and the frame superscripts compared with Craig.

## Conventions used
- Pose of end effector from an ETS (Rz, tx, …), not DH, in this week. Frames {0}, {1}, … numbered along the chain; $^A\mathbf R_B$ notation, $^0\boldsymbol\nu$ = twist in {0}.
- Twist ordering: **linear first, angular second** (υ above ω), 6×n J; augmented skew likewise (s₁-s₃ linear, s₄-s₆ angular).
- Default Jacobian is in the base frame {0} ("jacob0"); `jacobe` gives the end-effector frame (used for the singularity example, p. 45).
- Joint velocity limits, angles: degrees given in exercises, radians in code. Planar RR: q₂ measured relative to link 1; Greek υ = linear, ω = angular, ν = both.
- Manipulability m uses square roots of eigenvalues of A = J Jᵀ (p. 55); reciprocal definition noted as alternative.
- Singularity classification: boundary vs interior (p. 44). Slide says "Jacobian ... singular, det = 0"; for non-square J (6×n, n≠6) det is not defined, use pseudo-inverse (p. 42) and rank/eigenvalue of J Jᵀ.

## Likely exam angles
Velocity kinematics is not one of the listed sample archetypes (Q1-Q10), so expect a new question of derive/calculate/explain type:
- **Calculate**: Jacobian of a planar RR (or 3R) arm at a given q, in frame {0} or end-effector frame (use the p. 40 rotation); det J = L₁L₂ sin q₂; joint rates from desired Cartesian velocity (q̇ = J⁻¹ν), resolved-rate update q ← q + q̇Δt (Q4 pattern); joint torques from a tip force (τ = Jᵀ F).
- **Derive**: derive J by differentiating FK (pp. 33-34) or by the ETS method (steps 1-4); show Ṙ = [ω]ₓR so [ω]ₓ = ṘRᵀ; show why ellipsoid has JJᵀ.
- **Sketch**: arm at q₂ = 0° and 180°; velocity ellipsoid near and far from a singularity; force ellipsoid as reciprocal of velocity ellipsoid.
- **Explain/spot the error**: boundary vs interior singularity; why joint velocities blow up near singularity; frame of J (a Jacobian "in {0}" vs "in {E}"); velocity ellipsoid uses (JJᵀ)⁻¹, force ellipsoid uses JJᵀ; m=1 isotropic, m=0 singular; non-square J needs pseudo-inverse; Jᵀ always exists even when J⁻¹ does not; at a singularity the arm can resist large forces in the degenerate direction (standard result, not on slides).
- Mobility link: rank of J relates to degrees of freedom the end effector can instantaneously move in.
