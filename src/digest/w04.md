## Formulas

**Basic Grübler–Kutzbach (p. 25, slide image)**
$$M = D\,(N - J - 1) + \sum_{i=1}^{J} F_i$$
M = mobility/DoF; D = 6 (3D) or 3 (2D); N = number of links INCLUDING the grounded link; J = number of joints; F_i = DoF of joint i (R=1, P=1, U=2, S=3). Equivalent to D(N−1) − (D−F)·J form. Source: slide image.

**Extended GK (p. 35, slide image)**
$$M = D\,(N - J - 1) + \sum_{i=1}^{J} F_i + R_C - R_M,\qquad R_C=\sum_{i=1}^{J}F_i-\sum_{k=1}^{L}u_k$$
R_C = number of over-constraints; R_M = local (internal, redundant) mobility, i.e. DoF that do not move the end-effector; u_k = unique DoFs of the platform contributed by closed loop k (union counted once); L = number of loops considered. Source: slide image.

**Four-bar loop closure, link 2 broken at K (p. 54, image).** a, b = distances from K to the two ends of link 2 (L₂ = a + b). Frames {S} at the left base, {D} at the right base, L₀ = base distance (x_S to x_D). Active joint θ₁; passive φ₁, φ₂, φ₃. Angles in radians.
$$^{S}x_K=L_1\cos\theta_1+a\cos(\theta_1+\varphi_2),\quad ^{S}y_K=L_1\sin\theta_1+a\sin(\theta_1+\varphi_2)$$
$$^{D}x_K=L_3\cos\varphi_1+b\cos(\varphi_1+\varphi_3),\quad ^{D}y_K=L_3\sin\varphi_1+b\sin(\varphi_1+\varphi_3)$$
Rigidity of the broken link (3 independent equations, n=1, m=3, J=4):
$$L_1\cos\theta_1+a\cos(\theta_1+\varphi_2)=L_0+L_3\cos\varphi_1+b\cos(\varphi_1+\varphi_3)$$
$$L_1\sin\theta_1+a\sin(\theta_1+\varphi_2)=L_3\sin\varphi_1+b\sin(\varphi_1+\varphi_3)$$
$$\theta_1+\varphi_2=\varphi_1+\varphi_3+\pi$$

**Freudenstein-type constraint, break at both ends of link 2 (p. 57, image)** — U on link 1 end, V on link 3 end:
$$^{S}x_U=L_1\cos\theta_1,\ ^{S}y_U=L_1\sin\theta_1;\quad ^{D}x_V=L_3\cos\varphi_1,\ ^{D}y_V=L_3\sin\varphi_1$$
$$\eta_1(\theta_1,\varphi_1)=(L_1\cos\theta_1-L_0-L_3\cos\varphi_1)^2+(L_1\sin\theta_1-L_3\sin\varphi_1)^2-L_2^2=0$$
One equation, n=1, m=1, J=4. Slide calls it "the Freudenstein equation". Note: slide writes ^D x_V without the L₀ offset in the frame, then subtracts L₀ in η₁.

**Four-bar FK (pp. 62, 64, 65, image).** Given x ≡ ^S x_K, y ≡ ^S y_K, δ = θ₁ + φ₂:
$$A_1\cos\delta+B_1\sin\delta+C_1=0,\ A_1=x-L_0,\ B_1=y,\ C_1=\tfrac{1}{2b}\left[(x-L_0)^2+y^2+b^2-L_3^2\right]$$
$$A_2\cos\delta+B_2\sin\delta+C_2=0,\ A_2=x,\ B_2=y,\ C_2=\tfrac{1}{2a}\left[L_1^2-a^2-x^2-y^2\right]$$
(Eq. 1 uses x−L₀ = L₃cosφ₁ − b cos δ; eq. 2 uses x = L₁cosθ₁ + a cos δ. Both derived by squaring and adding.) Slide p. 65: "use tangent half angle substitution and solve":
$$\delta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{A_1C_2-A_2C_1}{(B_1C_2-B_2C_1)+(A_1B_2-A_2B_1)}\right),\quad (A_1B_2-A_2B_1)^2=(A_1C_2-A_2C_1)^2+(B_1C_2-B_2C_1)^2$$
Simplified consistency condition (slide p. 65, transcribed; numerically verified as an identity):
$$4a^2b^2L_0^2y^2=\big[b(x-L_0)(L_1^2-a^2-x^2-y^2)-ax((x-L_0)^2+y^2+b^2-L_3^2)\big]^2+y^2\big[b(L_1^2-a^2-x^2-y^2)-a((x-L_0)^2+y^2+b^2-L_3^2)\big]^2$$
Recovery (p. 66): φ₂ = δ − θ₁; φ₁ from η₁(θ₁,φ₁)=0; φ₃ = θ₁ + φ₂ − φ₁ − π.

**Four-bar IK, output pose (x, y, φ) of link 2 at K given (p. 70–72, image).** φ = orientation of output link.
$$x=L_1\cos\theta_1+a\cos(\theta_1+\varphi_2),\ y=L_1\sin\theta_1+a\sin(\theta_1+\varphi_2),\ \phi=\theta_1+\varphi_2-2\pi$$
$$\theta_1=\operatorname{atan2}(y-a\sin\phi,\ x-a\cos\phi),\qquad \varphi_2=\phi-\theta_1\ (+2\pi\text{ crossed out on slide})$$
Right side (p. 72): x = L₀ + L₃cosφ₁ + b cos(φ₁+φ₃); y = L₃ sinφ₁ + b sin(φ₁+φ₃); φ = φ₁+φ₃−π. "Solve for φ₁ and φ₃" (left as an exercise on the slide). Note L₁ is eliminated since atan2 is used on the vector (x−a cosφ, y−a sinφ) which has length L₁ only if the pose is reachable.

## Worked examples and numbers on the slides
- p. 18 payload-to-mass: DLR KUKA-arm-based simulator mass 4.7 t, payload 1.3 t; BAA Training Stewart-based simulator mass 1.8 t, payload 10.0 t (as printed).
- p. 16: BRL fracture-surgery tool 120 µm precision, load 36 kg (belongs to the left, BRL picture). Taylor spatial frame, Mazor spine device have no numbers.
- p. 25–27 **Planar 3-PRR parallel manipulator** (3 legs each R at ground, P in the middle, R at platform; triangular platform; D=3). Instructor's handwriting: N = 8, J = 9 (a "7" was crossed out). Links: ground + 6 leg links (cylinder, piston per leg) + platform = 8. Joints: 3 R + 3 P + 3 R = 9. All F_i=1 so ΣF=9. M = 3(8 − 9 − 1) + (9) = 3.
- p. 29–30 **Simplest Delta** (3 legs: active R at base, then U joint, link, U joint to platform; passive joints: two U per leg). N = 8, J = 9. M = 6(8 − 9 − 1) + (1+2+2)×3 = 3. Note on slide: "the mechanism is still 3D despite this depiction".
- p. 31: simplest Delta not used in practice: not stiff enough; play-free universal joints are hard to find; play lowers frequency of operation.
- p. 32–33 **Common Delta** (each leg R then parallelogram of two parallel bars joined by 4 S joints (2 at top, 2 at bottom), platform). N = 11 (base 1, 3 upper arms, 6 parallelogram bars, platform 1; handwritten 1…11), J = 15 (3 R + 12 S). M = 6(11 − 15 − 1) + (1 + 4×3)×3 = 9.
- p. 34: 9 DoF but only 3 affect the platform: 6 supplementary DoF are internal, rotations of the parallel bars about their long axis.
- p. 36 **Extended GK on common Delta**: M = 6(11 − 15 − 1) + (1+4×3)×3 + 0 − 6 = 3. So R_C = 0, R_M = 6.
- p. 37–43 **Parallel 4RRR** (two square platforms, 4 identical RR legs, 12 revolute joints; handwritten link labels 1…10 with 1 = base, 2 = moving platform, 3–10 = eight leg links). N = 10, J = 12, ΣF = 12. D = 6.
  - First pass: M = 6(10 − 12 − 1) + 12 + R_C − R_M = −6 + R_C − R_M (p. 38; handwritten R_M → 0).
  - Loop 1 (two legs on one side) platform DoF: T_y, T_z, R_x (p. 39). Loop 2 (other pair of legs): T_x, T_z, R_y (p. 40).
  - Unique DoFs: loop 1 {T_y, T_z, R_x}; loop 2 {T_x, T_z, R_y}; T_z shared, so Σu_k = 5 (p. 41).
  - R_C = ΣF_i − Σu_k = 12 − 5 = 7; R_M = 0; M = −6 + 7 − 0 = 1 (p. 42). Slide p. 43 states "the mechanism indeed has only 1 DoF".
- p. 45: q = (θ, φ); θ = active set of n joints; φ = passive set of m joints; each angle ∈ [0, 2π).
- p. 49 recipe for loop closure: break the manipulator into serial chains, get pose of the break point for each chain, rejoin with the joint constraint at the break. Choose breaks so that m (number of passive variables solved) is least and the minimum number of constraint equations η_i(q)=0, i∈1..m, is used.
- p. 50: planar four-bar, one loop, frames {S},{1},{2},{3},{4},{D}; θ₁ active; φ₁, φ₂, φ₃ passive. Handwritten: "active motor θ₁ = 30°" (illustrative; no other numbers).
- p. 58 Gough–Stewart: 6 active (prismatic) and 30 passive parameters; many loops; each loop potentially has 6 independent equations; choosing which 30 is not trivial. (Legs: P active; S and U passive; 6 legs × (3 + 2) = 30 passive DoF.)
- p. 60 FK steps: (1) get m loop-closure equations in m passive + n active variables, solve the non-linear system for φ_i; (2) compute pose of output link from θ_i and φ_i. "There is no general method like in the case of serial manipulators."
- p. 67: more complex mechanisms: multiple solutions may exist, some solutions may have imaginary values for passive joints, numerical approaches may be used.
- p. 69: IK given pose of chosen output link: determine active and passive joint variables; simpler than FK, no loop-closure equations needed; break into serial chains and solve each; make sure each serial chain is not redundant (else no unique solution).
- p. 74 comparison table: Serial vs Parallel — open chains vs closed chains; joints active vs active and passive; FK simple vs complex; IK complex vs simple; joint errors cumulative vs non-cumulative; work volume large vs small; payload-to-weight low vs very high; inertia of moving parts high vs low.
- p. 75 summary: parallel robots have large payloads and are more accurate; typically more joints than DoF; FK not easy; IK easier.

- Workspace definitions (p. 21): Reachable: points that end-effector can reach, orientation arbitrary. Dexterous: point the EE can reach in all its possible orientations. Constant orientation workspace: points reachable in a given orientation. Orientation workspace: all possible orientations at a given location.
- Workspace methods: (p. 22) discretisation: grid formed by moving each actuator a small amount, boundary of node set = workspace; drawback: increment size limits accuracy, slow. (p. 23) geometry: use min/max of each actuator's limits and take all physical constraints into account.

## Conventions used
- Basic GK uses N including ground ("one of which is grounded"). Handwritten note on p. 29 reads "M = D(N−J+1) + ΣF_i": **differs from printed equation; sign of the 1 is wrong in the note** (D(N−J−1) is correct and gives M=3 on p. 30).
- Frames {S} (source/base at left pivot) and {D} (destination/right pivot) for the four-bar; {1}…{4} are link frames; ^S x_K means K expressed in {S}. L₀ is the distance between the pivots along x_S. Angles θ (active) and φ (passive) are absolute rotations about z measured counter-clockwise; link frame x axes point along the link. The link-2 angle uses φ₃ measured from x₃ so that the +π appears in θ₁+φ₂ = φ₁+φ₃+π.
- Output link orientation is called φ (italic phi, p. 70 onward) and is distinct from passive angles φ_i.
- No DH parameters, no ᴬT_B transformations appear except ^B T_P in the homework.
- Joint DoFs used: R = 1, P = 1, U = 2, S = 3. D = 3 for planar, 6 for spatial.
- Joint labels on slides: R revolute, P prismatic, U universal (Cardan), S spherical.

## Uncertain / disagreements
