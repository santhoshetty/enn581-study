\({}^A\mathbf R_B\), \({}^A\mathbf t_B\)its rotation (columns = B’s axes in A) and translation (B’s origin in A)

Exam technique (written, 2 hours, printed notes)
  • Calculator in radians unless a question is in degrees. Write the unit of every angle.
  • Draw frames first. Most FK, IK, Jacobian and dynamics errors are sign errors that a labelled sketch prevents.
  • State the general equation, then substitute (ETS, cosine rule, Euler–Lagrange, G–K, cubic boundary conditions). This is where method marks are.
  • Use atan2 and give every IK branch. Say how many solutions exist and when there are none.
  • Order matters: right-multiply for moves about the current frame. Check the product at θ = 0 against the drawing.
  • Units: mm vs m in link lengths; rad/s vs rpm in wheels; °/s in trajectories.
  • Say what you assume (“no joint limits”, “point masses”, “rest-to-rest”, “rollers ⊥ drive so γ = 0”).
  • Explain answers are 2–4 sentences: claim, reason, consequence (e.g. “det J = 0, so the arm loses a direction of motion; joint speeds blow up nearby”).
  • Stuck? Write the set-up and the equation you would solve. Partial credit is real. Move on, come back.
  • Booklet: start at its index (“if the question says … go to page …”). Don’t read; look up.
